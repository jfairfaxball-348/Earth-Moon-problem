## CLM-0071: complete one-edge swap theorem (hand proof)

Let `G=B[w]` be a clique blow-up of any finite simple base graph `B` with **all bag sizes at least two**. Let `e` be an edge such that `G-e` has a proper `q`-colouring `c`, and let `f=uv` be any nonedge of `G`. Then `G-e+f` also has a proper `q`-colouring.

Proof. Endpoints `u,v` of f lie in two distinct NONADJACENT base bags `A,B`: vertices in one bag are adjacent, and adjacent bags have complete cross-edges. The two endpoint bags of e cannot be precisely A and B, since then e would be absent. Hence one of A,B, say `W`, contains **neither endpoint of e**. Bag W contains at least two vertices. Because e has no endpoint in W, W remains a clique in G-e, so c gives its vertices distinct colours. If c(u)≠c(v), the colouring already extends. Otherwise, let z be the endpoint of f in W and choose another vertex z' in W. Transposing the colours at z,z' is a graph automorphism of G-e: inside W there is a clique; all outside vertices see either all of W or none; neither endpoint of deleted e is in W. The transposed colouring remains proper on G-e and makes u,v differently coloured. Thus it extends to G-e+f. QED.

## CLM-0072: up to five retained-Sulanke extension vertices cannot restore ten chromatic colours while remaining biplanar

Let `H=K6 vee C5` be the **11-vertex, 50-edge 9-vertex-critical** biplanar Sulanke graph. For r in 0,...,5, form any finite simple graph G on V(H) and r new vertices with H as the **induced graph on its original eleven vertices** (old-old edges unchanged). If G is biplanar, then chi(G)<=9.

For r=0, chi(H)=6+3=9. For r=1: if the new vertex x misses any old u, the 8-colourability of H-u (criticality) gives a 9-colouring of H+x in which u and x both have ninth colour; all remaining old vertices take the eight old colours. Thus chi(H+x)>=10 forces x universal to H. Such a graph has 12 vertices and 50+11=61 edges, exceeding `6·12-12=60`, the maximum two-layer planar edge count. Hence r=1 cannot be biplanar and chi>=10 simultaneously.

For r>=2, induct on r. Suppose a biplanar G with chi(G)>=10 existed. Removing any of the r new vertices gives a biplanar graph retaining induced H plus r-1 new vertices; by induction, this deletion is 9-colourable. Hence each new vertex in G has degree **at least 9**, since otherwise its colour could be supplied from the nine colours after deletion. Let a denote edges from H to new vertices, b edges within the r new vertices, and t=a+b. Then sum of degrees of new vertices is `a+2b=t+b >= 9r`. Since `b<=binom(r,2)`, obtain `t>=9r-binom(r,2)`. Biplanarity gives `50+t<=6(11+r)-12=54+6r`, so `t<=4+6r`. These inequalities conflict precisely when `3r-binom(r,2)>4`, which holds for r=2,3,4,5 (values5,6,6,5). Contradiction; no such biplanar 10-chromatic G. QED.

The premise is **induced H**: adding missing old-old edges, deleting old edges, edge swaps, vertex splitting, different nine-critical cores or six-plus new vertices are NOT eliminated.
