## CLM-0071 — general chromatic swap obstruction (complete hand proof)
Let G be a blowup of a simple base graph: every base vertex a is replaced by a nonempty clique B_a, each B_a is adjacent completely to B_b or anticomplete depending on base adjacency. Assume every |B_a|>=2. Let e be any original edge, f=xy any original nonedge, and c a proper q-colouring of G-e. Since f is a nonedge, x and y lie in two **distinct anticomplete bags** B_a and B_b. At least one of these two bags has NO endpoint of e: if e met both bags, e would join two distinct anticomplete bags and so could not be an original edge. Choose the untouched bag, say B_a, and its endpoint x. In G-e, all vertices of B_a remain adjacent true twins, hence each has a different colour from each other and exactly the same outside neighbours. If c(x)!=c(y), keep c. If c(x)=c(y), choose z in B_a distinct from x, and exchange the colours at x and z. This preserves all properness relations in G-e (the colour permutation exchanges two identically adjacent twin positions), and makes f bichromatic because c(z)!=c(x)=c(y). So c becomes a proper q-colouring of G-e+f. Since adding f cannot lower chromatic number, chi(G-e+f)=chi(G-e). No planarity assumption used. The complete six-row finite check is an independent corroboration, not the basis for the general proof.

## CLM-0072 — universal apex filter
For n>=3, any biplanar graph has <=6n-12 edges. If H has n vertices and m edges, H joined to one universal vertex has n+1 vertices and m+n edges, so requires m<=5n-6. Sulanke's nine-chromatic K6 join C5 has n=11, m=15+5+30=50; bound is49. Its apex is12 vertices,61 edges >60, so fails biplanarity by ONE edge. This cannot exclude less-dense nine-chromatic sources, multi-vertex engineered joins or any currently unknown original candidate. Filter only; not a new upper-bound theorem.

## CLM-0073 — adjacent vertex-splitting degree filter
Take a proper q-colouring of G and split v into adjacent v1,v2, allocating each original incidence to exactly one copy. Keep all other old colours and initially give v1 colour c(v). At least one copy has at most floor(deg(v)/2) old neighbours. For deg(v)<=2q-3, that is <=q-2. Give that copy a colour distinct from c(v) and distinct from the at most q-2 colours on its old neighbours; at least one of the q-1 alternatives exists. Colour the other copy c(v). Thus any such split remains q-colourable. For q=9 this excludes degree<=15. In Sulanke K6 join C5, clique-side vertices have degree10; C5-side vertices have degree8, so every adjacent one-vertex split is nine-colourable. This does not eliminate higher-degree vertices, multi-vertex splits, or added new adjacencies.
