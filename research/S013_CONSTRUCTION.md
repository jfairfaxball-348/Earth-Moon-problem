## CLM-0075: the K6 boundary is either nine-colourable or contains a K9

For internal K6, each added vertex has five internal neighbours and needs >=4 cross edges. Total cross incidences are24 or25; therefore at least five new vertices have EXACTLY four old neighbours and any remaining new vertex has four or five. Fix a proper nine-colouring c of H, giving six distinct colours to K6 and three separate colours to C5. Give each new vertex the allowed colours not used on its old neighbours. The five four-neighbour vertices have lists size >=5, the sixth size>=4. Every list-subfamily of size <=5 satisfies Hall; for a 5-subfamily at least four lists are size>=5, and for <=4 at least one list offers >=its size. Thus a nine-colour extension exists whenever the six lists have union >=6.

Assume, for contradiction, *no* H-colouring extends. In EVERY proper 9-colouring of H the union of the six allowed lists must have size<=5. Hence each of the >=5 four-old-neighbour vertices must forbid exactly four *distinct* colours and must forbid the SAME four colours. If one such neighbourhood in H is not a clique, it contains two nonadjacent C5 vertices (all K6 vertices are pairwise adjacent and joined to C5). Any nonadjacent pair of C5 vertices can share a colour in a proper 3-colouring of C5. Choose such a colouring and give the K6 six separate colours: that new vertex now forbids <=3 distinct colours and has >=6 allowed, contradicting the assumed Hall failure. Thus each four-old-neighbour set is a K4 of H.

If two of these K4 neighbourhoods differ, choose a 9-colouring of H distinguishing their *sets* of four colours: differing old-clique vertices always have distinct unique clique colours; if old-clique parts are identical but C5 parts differ, choose a C5 vertex in their symmetric difference and 3-colour C5 with that vertex the singleton colour class (possible at each of its five vertices). The two lists then differ, and their union has >=6 colours, again meeting Hall. So all five four-neighbour new vertices have the SAME four-vertex clique neighbourhood S in H. Those five vertices are a K5 inside the new K6 and are fully joined to S=K4; together they induce a K9. The classical/independently programme-certified K9 thickness>2 contradiction (CLM0022, SRC0014). Therefore G is 9-colourable if biplanar.
